# Evidence and data audit

| Question | Primary evidence | What it can and cannot establish |
|---|---|---|
| Did younger employment fall relative to older or less AI-exposed workers? | [Stanford ADP, Canaries](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publication/canaries-in-the-coal-mine-six-facts-about-the-recent-employment-effects-of-artificial-intelligence/) | Downloadable aggregate developer and age-by-exposure panels. Revised paper reports a 19% relative gap among 22–25-year-olds in exposed occupations through June 2026, mainly through reduced hiring. Age is not tenure; the published gap is relative, not an absolute fall or an identified causal effect. Raw payroll is not public. |
| Did posting language change? | [Burning Glass Institute / Break Through Tech, The Consequence Gap](https://www.burningglassinstitute.org/research/the-consequence-gap) | Over a million entry-level postings across 32 tech occupations. Review mentions rose from 7.8% in 2020 to 15.6% in 2025 and 18.5% in early 2026. Ads are neither representative observed time diaries nor causal evidence. |
| Did one AI-native employer's hiring and workflows change? | [Employment at Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/employment-at-anthropic) | Junior share of technical hires fell 27% to 9% while overall technical hiring grew; shares are not junior hire counts. Claude meta-work share grew in observed employee sessions, not specifically junior working time. |
| What have researchers directly observed? | [METR RCT and data](https://github.com/METR/Measuring-Early-2025-AI-on-Exp-OSS-Devs), [paper](https://metr.org/Early_2025_AI_Experienced_OS_Devs_Study-paper.pdf), [Developers as Managers of AI Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33588) | METR's published CSV includes implementation and post-review timing, but not its detailed video phase labels. Participants are experienced OSS developers. Another preprint shadows developers using agents; a small observational sample is not a junior pre/post comparison. |
| Does AI change how much people can complete or what they learn? | [Cui et al. field experiments](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2025.00535), [Shen and Tamkin](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20245), [apprentice experiment](https://iza.org/en/publications/dp/18999/task-expansion-with-generative-ai-experimental-evidence-on-middle-skilled-early-career-workers) | These answer different questions. In the 52-participant library-learning study, immediate comprehension scores were 50% versus 67%; successful usage subgroups were tiny and post hoc. The 673-apprentice study measured immediate comprehension, not durable transfer. |
| Does judged expertise predict agent outcomes? | [Anthropic Claude Code study](https://www.anthropic.com/research/claude-code-expertise) | Transcript-classified task-specific expertise and verified success are not independently measured worker seniority or production quality. |

The most valuable missing data: chronological time records that link human task phase, agent access, and **actual career tenure**, with independent performance and delayed learning measures. Do not use job-posting frequencies to calibrate human time shares. A historical benchmark and a randomized contemporary pilot answer different questions.
