# Tracking changes in entry-level STEM postings

This is the early-career slice of [A New Shape for Work](https://github.com/nikbearbrown/a-new-shape-for-work/blob/main/paper/manuscript.md), not a separate taxonomy. Start from observed technical job postings, not syllabi. Include software, QA, data and AI, systems and security, physical engineering, applied science, and technical implementation, product, or program roles when duties warrant it. A master's degree is neither required for inclusion nor sufficient evidence that a role is entry level.

## Identify the cohort before measuring change

- Retain three labels: description-supported entry level, title-only lead, and unclear. An explicit new-graduate route or a required experience range compatible with entry level is stronger evidence than “associate” alone. Record required and preferred qualifications separately. Keep internships and co-ops outside the main cohort. Review ambiguous cases and a sample of both accepted and rejected postings to detect missed roles.
- Assign job family by duties, keep the original title for audit, and preserve the occupational code and coding confidence separately. Do not infer the work phase from the job title or code.
- Reuse the parent paper's six work phases: Understand, Plan, Produce, Check, Sustain, and Coordinate. Code what duties the employer describes and distinguish that from qualifications. Keep AI-tool requirements, degree and experience requirements, and explicit training or mentorship as separate fields. An ad that does not mention a duty or mentorship does not establish that it is absent.

## Make the comparison interpretable

Use one observation per distinct posting and distinguish newly observed postings from the stock of open ads. For the first comparison, show four or more complete weekly cohorts from the same observed boards under a fixed eligibility and coding rubric. Where available, compare against a source-matched historical period and check whether the result survives restricting to the same employers and job families. Report coverage, deduplication, ambiguous cases, and the number of eligible postings behind each share. Show counts and shares within families so a shift in the mix of employers or roles is not mistaken for a change in what a given role asks for.

The nightly filter bootstraps candidate collection. A title-only baseline describes what the filter found, not the confirmed composition of entry-level STEM, and one snapshot cannot show a trend. Publish methods and reviewable aggregates here; keep posting-level extracts in the private collection.

## Keep the claims separate

A change in job ads is a change in stated demand in the covered sources, not evidence that employers hired different people, that workers spent more time in a given phase, or that AI caused the change. The proposed [direct work-measurement pilot](phase-measurement.md) addresses human effort and skill formation in software as one setting, not as a proxy for the entire STEM cohort.
